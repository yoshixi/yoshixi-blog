I have worked close to founders and CEOs since my early twenties.  
Compared to many peers, I often feel my environment is unusual.

## You start thinking from the final decision-maker's perspective

Working near the final approver changes how you see decisions.

The pressure is simply different between:

- people who are delegated decisions
- people whose own survival depends on those decisions

Understanding that pressure has been one of the biggest benefits of my current environment.

## It becomes harder to fully sympathize with complaints from large-company contexts

When I hear frustrations from purely player-level positions, I often also see the operational burden on management.

This does not mean employee concerns are invalid.  
It means mutual understanding across layers is necessary:

- player
- manager
- executive

## I feel my practical resilience has improved

Working in less-structured environments forced me to build "survival strength."  
Even if one company fails, I feel I can keep working in many other settings.

## Overall

I enjoy working in environments that are not fully systematized yet.  
It can be messy, but it is deeply educational and often exciting.
